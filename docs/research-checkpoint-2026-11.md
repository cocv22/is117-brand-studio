**IS117 T-shirt Research | November 2026**

This checkpoint records brand and audience hypotheses, observations from three clothing sites, and questions raised in an **AI-assisted feedback conversation that the student reports the instructor approved for this activity**. It does not represent independent customer interviews, a human-classmate review, or a completed commercial store.

## Positioning alternatives

- **A — The Resilient Achiever (selected primary position):** A broad audience facing ordinary setbacks or difficult periods and looking for encouragement. Intended reaction: “I needed that reminder. I can keep going.”
- **B — The Individual with a Purpose (secondary influence):** Someone who wants clothing to express determination, identity, and ambition. Intended reaction: “This represents who I am. I refuse to give up.”

The shared value is **perseverance**. A customer should not need to be visibly successful or confident to belong. Both positions are hypotheses, not validated market segments.

## Two persona hypotheses

1. **Person Facing One Setback After Another:** Repeated difficulties can be discouraging. A wearable visual message may offer a small reminder of hope and the possibility of taking another step. The clothing does not solve the underlying problem.
2. **Goal-Driven Overcomer:** Someone working toward personal growth, confidence, discipline, or independence may want a shirt as both a reminder and a statement of their mindset.

**Chosen design direction:** Expressive, bold shirt designs framed by a clean, easy-to-navigate site. The working identity uses strong typography, sharp imagery, recurring kanji concepts, and a coherent visual language. Product concepts are not claims of commercial availability. Exact kanji meanings/connotations need qualified review before commercial use.

## Brand character and honest persuasion

- **Character:** Hero/resilience; encouraging, determined, confident, and empathetic.
- **Voice:** Supportive without being passive; bold without hostility.
- **Customer promise:** Clothing for self-expression and a symbolic reminder to keep moving forward — **not** a guarantee of personal success.
- **Trust:** Accurate photos, descriptions, sizing and policies, when verified. Do not invent material details, interviews, testimonials, scarcity or reviews.
- **Site experience:** Keep navigation simple and product selections clear. The existing website is a _classroom demo_; it does not accept orders or payments.

## Peer challenge, response and disposition

The student presented the two audience mindsets and competitor observations in an AI-assisted discussion; the student reports that the instructor approved using this discussion for the class feedback activity.

| Challenge raised                                                  | Student response                                        | Decision / next step                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Will one brand work for someone struggling and someone confident? | A simple common message and design can reach both.      | **Implement in documentation:** Make perseverance the common thread; avoid exclusivity.                |
| How can varied designs stay recognizable?                         | Repeat kanji, sharp artwork and succinct mottos.        | **Investigate:** Keep recurring cues; explore limited customization, not yet implemented.              |
| What would create first-visit product trust?                      | Credible interviews and detailed material descriptions. | **Defer unsupported claims:** Provide only actual product facts and authentic evidence when available. |
