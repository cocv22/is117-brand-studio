## Product boundary

The product is the same white T-shirt throughout the comparison. The learner first agreed to offer FLY and FIGHT as selectable design options, then requested a DREAM / 夢 option to offer hope or prompt reflection on past aspirations. All three share the same price and size selector. Kanji color options are white, crimson, gold, and sky blue; the circle remains black. Colored inks have a shiny or matte finish option; white remains matte. The classroom cart records design, color, finish, and size, shows a total, and allows individual items to be removed. No real orders, payment, accounts, or persistence. Keep product facts stable between directions; do not add unsupported claims about materials, manufacturing, sustainability, fit, delivery, scarcity, reviews, or sales.

## Ethical persuasion

Choose two applications that help the visitor make a voluntary, informed decision. Record their real implementation and mechanism rather than a principle name alone. Required size selection or validation alone does not establish consistency. Do not invent social proof, scarcity, authority, or product facts.

| Principle   | Actual copy or interaction                                                                                     | Visitor benefit / factual support                                                                                             |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reciprocity | Free FLY/FIGHT/DREAM context lines appear before design choice; no purchase or sign-up is needed to read them. | Gives shoppers clear design context; the audience associations are the learner's hypotheses, not validated research.          |
| Consistency | Shopper voluntarily selects a mark and color; adding the shirt carries those choices into the cart.            | The cart reflects the shopper's prior choice. The voluntary design selection, not required size selection, is the commitment. |

## CART-001 — Clear cart state and item removal

**Scope:** label the interaction as a cart, show an empty state with a link back to shopping, display added shirts and the total, and allow removing an individual shirt. Show one selected shirt design at a time; provide a “Choose symbol” control beside size and display the product price to two decimal places. Add DREAM / 夢 as a third design option, place DREAM above the symbol, and show the context line “To the dreams you have dreamed.” The learner says this option can offer hope or prompt reflection on past aspirations. Keep it temporary and demo-only; checkout remains out of scope.

Learner explained that selecting DREAM changes the shirt logo and wording like the other designs, giving it a different meaning that may appeal more to a customer.
